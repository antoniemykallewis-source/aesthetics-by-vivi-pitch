02 / The opportunityPublic-page review / October 20, 2026

The details should be as considered as the work.

There is already useful content here. The opportunity is to make attribution, service proof and the practitioner’s voice work together with more precision.

Review presentation01

Two names. The same review text.

On the homepage, the Emily F. and Hailey W. cards repeat the same wording. Hailey W. also shares an outbound review URL with Yasmeen H. This is a publishing attribution issue to verify, not a conclusion about the underlying reviews.

Homepage testimonial cards / names and link targets ↗
Vessel would

Check every testimonial against its original review, then correct the name, quote and source link together. Keep the display tied to a verified source and a current review-date check.

Treatment relevance02

Biostimulator proof talks about lips.

The Biostimulator page’s Success Stories includes testimonials about lip treatments. They may demonstrate care, but they do not explain the outcome of the service the visitor is researching.

Biostimulator page / Success Stories section ↗
Vessel would

Pair each priority treatment with relevant, consented case context or a practitioner explanation. Label general care testimonials clearly when treatment-specific proof is unavailable.

Brand consistency03

Facebook links take two routes.

The site links to both a Facebook people-page address and the legacy /botox-by-vivi/ address. We have not established whether Facebook resolves them to one current profile.

Homepage / header and footer Facebook destinations ↗
Vessel would

Confirm the preferred official Facebook destination and standardize the header, footer and profile links. Use clear link labels instead of the top-bar “Click here.”

Bring the explanation into the page

The Instagram widget already links to Reels. Add a deliberately placed practitioner video beside priority treatment information instead of relying on visitors to discover the social feed.

Make proof treatment-specific

The gallery already names treatment categories. Extend that useful structure with approved timing, goals and context rather than a larger pile of unexplained images.

Keep the details current

The footer shows © 2025 and the site uses a static “more than 250” review statement. Establish an upkeep cadence; verify the live Google profile before changing any count.

Evidence before assumptions

A specific observation.
A practical next step.

Every primary finding above points to the page reviewed. These observations describe the sampled public pages and browser session. They do not establish lost inquiries, campaign performance or every visitor’s experience.

What we would validate before scaling

Mobile speed + usability

Benchmark the current site on a controlled mobile profile, then test image loading, navigation and the first useful action. No fresh Lighthouse or Core Web Vitals score is asserted in this proposal.

Google profile + reviews

Confirm the owner-approved name, address, hours and booking link against the live profile. Review recent feedback and response handling. No directory count is presented as a fresh Google-verified total.

Social content + dates

Review the linked official profiles, recent post dates, formats and useful engagement with account access. A quiet website blog or missing embedded video does not establish that social content is inactive.

Ads + measurement

Inspect current campaign ownership, conversion definitions and consent behavior. Public page tags alone cannot prove active spend, missing tracking or successful attribution. Preserve working tools and test the actual handoff.

Search + AI discovery

Use Search Console and a repeatable local query set to establish a baseline. Improve practitioner, service and case context. We do not infer ranking failure or universal AI absence from a single search snapshot.

Booking + follow-up

With the team, test an agreed inquiry from click through confirmation and staff response. This public review did not submit patient information, place calls or make appointments.

The first phase confirms access, approvals and measurement before any paid launch. Proposed work and filming remain subject to the practice’s clinical review, patient permissions, existing vendor responsibilities and an agreed scope.
